Component description

This model represents the injection pre-chamber for the thrust engine (which can be seen in the figures from example1). The component is specifically designed to host several double swirl type injectors for flows in coaxial cones. The configuration is specifically designed for a propulsion architecture which includes turbopump machineries for both propellants. 

In particular, the interface with the oxidizer is represented by the large cavity on top and in the middle, surrounded by the upper flange. The oxidizer flow is intended to come directly from the turbine placed on top of the engine. The turbo-machinery complex for the fuel is supposed to be shifted from the axis of the engine. Its inlet interface is represented by the lateral rotating duct converging into the external volute of the model.

Initially a different type of inlet was used for the pre-injection chamber, meant for a simpler architecture where the fuel, after the regenerative cooling cycle, flows directly into the pre-chamber, and then injected through the injectors in the combustion chamber. The inlet interface for the fuel was characterized by the circular cavity surrounding the injection plate, which can be still seen in the current model. This feature and the volute type of inlet will be selectable by means of a switch in the future iterations. 

The component is not meant to work by itself alone. The injectors, which number can be chosen, are separate components that need to be threaded into the main part from the bottom side of the injection plate by means of the threaded surface on the internal surface of the cylinders. The thread has not been implemented into the model yet. The injectors are screwed inside the model until they reach the conical surfaces in the central part of the prechamber. By means of two o-rings, the injectors will isolate the lower part of the pre-chamber (under the first set of cones, from the combustion chamber and from the upper part of the pre-chamber. This way the flows of the two propellants are kept separated. The injectors then protrude inside the upper part of the model and they will feature two sets of radial injections holes in the upper part and lower part fo the model. The design of the single injectors will be done in the next iterations. The reason for this specific type of injection configuration comes from a previous design in a different project. I thought about the idea to re-use my idea in this project.

Few details are missing for the current model, such as the pressure transducer interface ports, the thread inside the cylindrical ports for the injectors, the flange at the tip of the fuel inlet, the O-ring cavity on the upper flange. The cavity on the lower flange cannot be created through AM, hence it needs to be machined. Therefor, for this reason, it will not be introduced in the future applications. Moreover, one additional feature which still needs to be implemented is a supporting rib under the fuel inlet duct. Since it is extruded horizontally from the external volute initially, the maximum overhang angle is not respect for that specific part of the component. Therefor, a slim and light rib will be introduced under the duct in order to connect the suspended volume to the horizontal plane. After the AM, the rib can be removed.
